## What are data models and why are they useful?
* Models are abstractions of real-world objects or events.
* Models help us to understand real-world complexities.
* Data models are simple representations of complex real-world data structures and they are often graphical.

---

## What are the basic building blocks of a data model?
* **Entity:** Anything about the data we are collecting.
* **Attribute:** A characteristic of the entity.
* **Relationships:** Describes the association between entities.
* There are 3 types of relationships, which are 1:M (one-to-many), M:M (many-to-many), and 1:1 (one-to-one).
* **Constraints:** Restrictions which are placed on the data.

---

## What are business rules and how do they translate into data models?
* These are the descriptions of the policies, procedures, and principles in the organizational environment.
* Business rules can be translated into data model components.
* Nouns are translated into entities.
* Verbs are translated into relationships between entities.
* Relationships are bidirectional.
* We can identify the type of relationship by asking how many instances of B are related to one instance of A (and vice versa).

---

## What is the Entity Relationship Model (ERM) and how is it structured?
* This is a widely accepted standard for data modeling.
* It is a graphical representation of entities and their relationships in a database structure.
* An example of this is an ERD (Entity Relationship Diagram).
* An **entity set** is a relational table.
* An **entity occurrence** is a row in the relational table.
* An **entity attribute** is a column in the relational table.
* **Connectivity** is just the type of relationship.
* **Cardinality** expresses the minimum or maximum number of entity occurrences associated with a single occurrence from a related entity.

---

## How can we summarize the core concepts of data modeling and business rules?
* Data models are abstractions of real-world complex environments.
* Business rules are used to define data modeling components such as entities, attributes, relationships, and constraints.
* The ERM model is a data modeling tool and is a widely accepted standard for data modeling.
